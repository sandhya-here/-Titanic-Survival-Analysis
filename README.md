# -Titanic-Survival-Analysis
## Project Overview
This project analyzes the famous Titanic dataset to uncover patterns and factors that influenced passenger survival rates during the disaster. Using Python data science libraries, the analysis explores demographic, socioeconomic, and other variables to identify key survival determinants.

## Key Findings

1. **Gender and Survival**: Women had a significantly higher survival rate than men, likely due to the "women and children first" policy implemented during evacuation.

2. **Class and Survival**: First-class passengers had higher survival rates than those in second and third class, suggesting socioeconomic status played a crucial role in survival chances.

3. **Age Distribution**: The majority of passengers were young adults, with children having better survival rates overall compared to other age groups.

4. **Family Size Impact**: Passengers traveling with small families (1-3 family members) had better survival rates than those traveling alone or with large families.

5. **Missing Data Patterns**: The "Cabin" column had the highest percentage of missing values, limiting some analyses related to passenger location on the ship.

6. **Fare Distribution**: Fare prices showed significant outliers, particularly in first class, indicating extreme wealth disparities among passengers.

7. **Embarkation Point**: Passengers who boarded at Cherbourg (C) had better survival rates than those from Southampton (S) or Queenstown (Q).

## Conclusion
The Titanic disaster survival patterns reveal the social dynamics of the early 20th century, where gender, class, and age significantly influenced one's chances of survival. These findings highlight how emergency protocols and social hierarchies operated during this historical tragedy.
